Advertising and sponsorship may help pay for our journalism. Two rules govern all of it: you should always know when someone has paid for what you are reading, and paying never buys influence over what we report. These rules apply whenever we carry paid content, on the site, in {{ brand.newsletter_name }} and on social media.

## How paid content is labelled

- **Ad.** An advertisement in the advertiser's own words or design.
- **Sponsored.** A paid message from a sponsor, for example in the newsletter, written or approved by the sponsor.
- **Partner content.** Material created with or for a commercial partner, which the partner may have approved. It is not journalism and does not go through our editorial checks.
- **Affiliate links.** If we ever earn a commission when you buy something through a link, we say so beside the link.

Labels appear at the top, before the content starts, and are easy to see on a phone. Paid content is designed to look different from our stories, and never carries our evidence file, our "Why this matters" or "Evidence and limitations" sections, or any mark suggesting we have verified it. On social media we also use each platform's paid partnership tools.

If an organisation ever funds a strand of our journalism without any say over its content, we will name the funder on every story in that strand.

## Separation from editorial

- Sponsors and advertisers never see or approve editorial content before publication.
- They never choose which stories we cover, or how.
- We don't publish advertorial disguised as news. Paid material never goes through our news pipeline.
- When we report on a sponsor or advertiser, the story says so.
- The editor can refuse to place an ad or sponsor message beside a story where it would mislead readers or be insensitive.

These rules reflect the law as well as our standards. The Consumer Protection Act 2007 prohibits using editorial content to promote a product that a trader has paid for without making clear that it is a paid promotion. The ASAI Code requires marketing communications to be clearly identifiable as such, and we follow the guidance of the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC) and the ASAI on disclosing commercial content.

## Categories we decline

We don't accept advertising, sponsorship or partner content for:

- gambling and betting;
- tobacco, vaping and other nicotine products;
- payday and other high-cost lending;
- fossil-fuel greenwashing, meaning environmental claims by fossil-fuel producers that the evidence doesn't support;
- political advertising, including parties, candidates, referendum campaigns and issue campaigns;
- unproven health products and treatments.

We also decline anything misleading or unlawful, and any claim of progress that our own standards would reject as a story. We can turn down any advertiser, and we don't have to give reasons.

## Newsletter sponsorship

- No more than one sponsor block in each issue of {{ brand.newsletter_name }}.
- The block is labelled "Sponsored" at its start and set apart visually from the stories.
- It never appears inside a story item or above the first story.
- Sponsors never receive subscribers' email addresses or any other personal data about subscribers.

## Complaints about paid content

If you think paid content on {{ brand.name }} isn't clearly labelled, or breaks this policy, email {{ publisher.complaints_email }}. We handle it under our Complaints and right of reply process. You can also complain about an advertisement to the ASAI.
