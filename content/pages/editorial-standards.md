These standards are the editorial constitution of {{ brand.name }}. They apply to every story, newsletter item and social post, whether a person or an AI tool wrote the first draft. When a story can't meet them, we don't publish it.

## Story classes

Before drafting, every story is given one class. The class decides what evidence it needs and how it must be framed.

**A. Verified progress.** Something measurably improved. We prefer primary data: official statistics, registers and monitoring records. We report the before and after, the period covered and who measured it.

**B. A solution working.** A programme, policy, product or practice has produced measured outcomes. We report the outcomes and the limitations: scale, cost, who did the measuring, whether it has been independently evaluated, and whether it is likely to work elsewhere.

**C. Human achievement.** Something a person or group did, confirmed by a reliable record. It must be non-exploitative. We don't present anyone's disability, illness, poverty or grief as a spectacle for other people's inspiration.

**D. Breakthrough.** New research or discovery. We always distinguish early findings from proven outcomes. Animal, laboratory and early-stage trial results are labelled as such in the headline or first sentence. A result in mice is not a treatment for people. The class name is ours; the word "breakthrough" only appears in a headline when the evidence supports it.

**E. Constructive context.** Real progress inside a difficult situation, such as a death rate that is falling but still high. We keep the difficult context in, and near the top. We never tragedy-launder: we don't lead with a silver lining and bury the harm.

### What we reject

**F. Unverified PR claims.** Announcements, pledges, targets and self-reported results that haven't been independently confirmed. We hold them until they are corroborated. A promise is not progress.

**G. Viral anecdotes.** Stories that spread on social media without a verifiable record. We reject them unless we can verify them.

## Accuracy

- Every factual statement must trace to a passage in a source, recorded in the story's evidence file.
- Quotes appear only verbatim from a source we can link to. We never invent, compose, tidy up or reconstruct a quote, and AI tools are never permitted to generate one. Where we shorten a quote, the cut must not change its meaning.
- **Relative and absolute change.** "Risk halved" can mean a fall from 2 in 1,000 to 1 in 1,000. Where the source allows, we give the absolute figures alongside the relative change.
- **Sample sizes.** For studies, trials and surveys, we say how many people, animals or sites were involved.
- **Peer-review status.** We say whether research is peer reviewed, a preprint, a conference presentation or a company announcement.
- **Cause and correlation.** We don't say one thing caused another unless the source shows it.
- **Limitations.** Every story states what we don't know yet and what could change the picture.

## Headlines and language

The headline must be supported by the body, and the body by the evidence. If the body has to qualify the headline, the headline is wrong.

We don't use hype words such as "cure", "breakthrough", "first", "miracle", "revolutionary" or "game-changer" unless the evidence supports them exactly. "First" needs a source that establishes it. Our checks flag these words automatically for an editor to decide.

## Fairness

- We report progress whoever achieved it, regardless of their politics.
- When a story makes a significant critical claim about a named person or organisation, we ask them for a response before publication where practicable, and we offer a right of reply afterwards.
- We keep fact and assessment apart. "Why this matters" is our assessment of the evidence and reads as such.
- We name private individuals only when there is good reason, and stories involving private individuals or children always go to a human editor.

## Independence

The editor decides what we publish and how. Owners, advertisers, sponsors, sources and partners cannot direct individual editorial decisions. Nobody can pay for coverage, and paid material is labelled and kept apart from our journalism under our Sponsored content policy. People involved in editorial decisions declare their interests, as set out on our Ownership and independence page.

## Press Council of Ireland

We commit to the principles of the Press Council of Ireland's Code of Practice, including truth and accuracy, distinguishing fact and comment, fairness and honesty, respect for rights, privacy, protection of sources, court reporting, prejudice and children.

Membership of the Press Council requires at least one year of operation. We intend to apply as soon as we are eligible. Until then, complaints are handled through our own process, described on our Complaints and right of reply page.

## When we fall short

When we get something wrong, we correct it in the open, with a dated note, under our Corrections policy. We would rather not publish a story than publish one we can't stand behind.
