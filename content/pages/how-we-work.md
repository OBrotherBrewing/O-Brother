This page explains, in plain English, how a story gets from a source to your screen. AI tools help at several steps. A person makes the final decision on every story we currently publish.

Every story goes through the same stages, in the same order. If it fails at any stage, it stops there.

## 1. Discover

We monitor official and primary sources: statistics agencies, regulators, research journals, public bodies, international organisations, and material published under open licences that allow reuse. We also scan news outlets and other sites for leads, but a lead is only a starting point. It tells us where to look, not what is true.

Our automated fetcher identifies itself to the websites it visits, follows their robots.txt instructions and respects text-and-data-mining reservations. Our Sources and verification page explains how we rank sources and what we may do with each kind.

## 2. Extract each claim

For each candidate story, the system breaks the source material into individual factual claims: every number, date, name, place, quote and statement of cause and effect. Each claim is stored with the exact passage in the source that supports it. A claim without a supporting passage is dropped.

## 3. Corroborate

Claims that matter, such as figures, health or safety information, statements about cause and effect, and anything said about a named person or organisation, need either:

- two independent sources, or
- one official or peer-reviewed primary source, such as the statistics agency that produced the figure or the journal that published the study.

"Independent" means a separate origin. Ten websites repeating one press release count as one source. Social media posts, blogs, press releases and user-generated content can point us to a story, but they never count as corroboration. If a story can't be corroborated, it is held, not published with a caveat.

## 4. Draft from verified claims only

An AI model writes an original article using only the verified claims and their supporting passages. It is not given the text of other newsrooms' articles, and it is instructed not to add facts that aren't in the evidence. It also drafts the "Why this matters" and "Evidence and limitations" sections.

## 5. Check, then check again

Before anyone reviews the draft, two layers of checks run:

- **Machine checks.** Every number, date, name and quote in the draft is matched against the evidence. Quotes must match the source word for word. The draft is also checked for hype words such as "cure" or "first" and for wording that sits too close to any source.
- **A second, separate AI check.** A fresh review goes through the draft claim by claim and asks whether each one is supported by the evidence as stated, without overstatement or missing context.

Anything uncertain fails closed. If a check can't confirm a claim, the claim comes out or the story stops. A failed check is never overridden automatically.

## 6. Human review

A named editor, {{ publisher.editor_of_record }}, reviews every story before publication. The editor sees the draft, the evidence file and the result of every check, and examines the substance: the claims, the framing, the headline and the limitations. The editor can edit the story, send it back or reject it.

Some subjects always go to a person, whatever the checks say. That will remain true even if we ever allow low-risk stories to publish without prior review:

- health and medical claims
- legal matters
- financial matters
- crime
- children
- private individuals
- elections
- conflict
- allegations against anyone

## 7. Publish with the evidence showing

Every story shows:

- its sources, with links;
- **Why this matters**;
- **Evidence and limitations**;
- an evidence file listing each claim, the passage that supports it, and the source it came from, with the source's address, publisher and the time we retrieved it;
- a note on how the story was produced and who reviewed it.

## After publication

A story isn't finished when it is published. We re-check its sources 6 hours, 1 day, 7 days and 30 days after publication. If a source is corrected, changed or withdrawn, the story goes back to an editor.

Corrections are visible and dated. We never silently change a fact. Our Corrections policy explains how to report an error and how quickly we act.

## What we keep

For every story we log which AI model and version did each step, the instructions it was given, the result of every check and the editor's decision. That record lets us explain how any story was made and trace the cause when something goes wrong.
