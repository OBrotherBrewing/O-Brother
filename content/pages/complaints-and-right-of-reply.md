If you think we have broken our own standards, or you or your organisation features in a story and you want to respond, this page explains how. We take every complaint seriously and answer it in writing.

## How to complain

Email {{ publisher.complaints_email }}. It helps if you include:

- a link to the story, newsletter issue or post;
- which of our standards you think we broke, and how;
- what you would like us to do;
- any evidence, ideally a link to a primary source.

If your concern is a factual error, writing to {{ publisher.corrections_email }} is usually quickest, but we will deal with it whichever address you use. You can also write to us at {{ publisher.legal_name }}, {{ publisher.registered_address }}.

## What happens next

1. **We acknowledge** your complaint within two working days.
2. **The editor investigates.** That means reading your complaint, the story, its evidence file and the sources, and contacting you if anything needs clarifying. Where a complaint concerns accuracy, our Corrections policy targets also apply.
3. **We reply in writing**, normally within ten working days. If we need longer, we tell you why and when to expect an answer.

If we uphold your complaint, we explain what we will do: a correction, a clarification, publication of your reply, a retraction, an apology, or a combination. If we don't uphold it, we explain why.

## Right of reply

If we write about you or your organisation, you have a right to respond.

- **Before publication.** When a story makes a significant critical claim about a named person or organisation, we ask them for a response before publication where practicable.
- **After publication.** If a story contains significant criticism of you, or a fact you dispute, we offer you a right of reply promptly. Where it is fair to do so, we publish your reply, or a fair summary agreed with you, linked from the story.

A reply should address the story, be of reasonable length, and not make unlawful or defamatory statements about anyone else. We don't charge for publishing a reply, and we won't edit it in a way that changes its meaning.

## Escalation

If you aren't satisfied with the editor's response, reply asking for a review. A director of {{ publisher.legal_name }} who was not involved in the original decision, where possible, will look at the complaint again and give you a written answer.

## Press Council of Ireland and the Press Ombudsman

We commit to the principles of the Press Council of Ireland's Code of Practice. We are not yet members: membership requires at least one year of operation, and we intend to apply as soon as we are eligible. Until then, the Press Ombudsman cannot consider complaints about us, and our own process above is the route for complaints.

Once we are members, if you are not satisfied with our response, you will be able to take your complaint to the Press Ombudsman. We will update this page when that happens.

## Other routes

- **Your personal data:** you can complain to the Data Protection Commission at www.dataprotection.ie. See our Privacy notice.
- **An advertisement:** you can complain to the ASAI. See our Sponsored content policy.

## Legal letters

We take legal correspondence seriously. Please send it to {{ publisher.complaints_email }} and to our registered address, marked "Legal".

When we receive a legal letter, we acknowledge it, preserve the story, its evidence file and our production records, and review the matter with our legal advisers. We respond through them.

A legal letter does not stop us correcting a genuine error promptly. Equally, we don't remove accurate, fair reporting because of a legal threat alone.
