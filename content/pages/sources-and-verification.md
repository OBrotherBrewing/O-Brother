A story is only as good as its sources. This page explains how we rank them, how much evidence a claim needs, and how we respect the work and rights of other publishers.

## Source tiers

**Tier 1: primary and official.** Official data, peer-reviewed research, courts and regulators, public agencies, international bodies and institutional records. When the source is the body that produced the data or made the decision, a Tier 1 source can support a claim on its own.

**Tier 2: established news.** Wire services and established publishers that have a published corrections standard.

**Tier 3: specialist and local.** Reputable specialist and trade outlets, universities, NGOs and local newsrooms.

**Tier 4: leads only.** Social media posts, blogs, press releases and user-generated content. These can tell us where to look. They are never evidence on their own.

Two distinctions matter:

- A public body's statement about its own data or decisions is an official record and sits in Tier 1. A press release from a company, charity or campaign is a lead in Tier 4, and we look for the data behind it.
- A preprint is a primary source for what its authors report, but it has not been peer reviewed, and we always say so.

## Corroboration rules

A **consequential claim** is one that could mislead or cause harm if wrong: a figure, anything about health, safety or money, a statement that one thing caused another, a "first" or a "record", and anything said about what a named person or organisation did.

- A consequential claim needs **two independent sources**, or **one official or peer-reviewed primary source**.
- **Independent** means a separate origin. Several outlets repeating the same press release or wire report count as one source. So do several sources owned by the same organisation.
- Tier 4 material never counts towards corroboration.
- Where reliable sources disagree, we report the disagreement or hold the story.
- Minor, uncontroversial details, such as a job title, may rest on one reliable source.

If a story can't meet these rules, it is held until it can. It is not published with a caveat instead.

## What we may do with each source

- **Openly licensed material**, such as work under Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY 4.0), the Open Government Licence or public-domain government works, may be reused and adapted on that licence's terms. We credit the author, name the licence, link to it and say if we changed the material. Where a licence forbids commercial use or adaptation, as CC BY-NC and CC BY-ND licences do, we use only the facts.
- **An organisation's own announcements** give us facts, and we may quote a sentence or two with a link.
- **Other newsrooms' journalism** is a lead. We confirm the facts from primary sources, write our own report, and credit and link to the outlet that found the story. The text of another newsroom's article is never given to our drafting model.
- **Some sources are link-only.** We describe them in our own words and link to them, without processing their full text.

## Respecting other publishers' rights

We never paraphrase another publisher's article wholesale. Beyond copyright itself, Irish law gives press publishers a right over online use of their publications, under the European Union (Copyright and Related Rights in the Digital Single Market) Regulations 2021 (S.I. No. 567/2021). We use no more than individual words or very short extracts of press publications, and we link to the original.

Rights holders can reserve their content from text and data mining. We honour those reservations, whether they are expressed through robots.txt, the TDM Reservation Protocol (TDMRep) or a website's terms. We don't bypass paywalls, logins or other access controls.

## Anonymous sources

At launch, we don't use anonymous sources. Every claim in a story traces to a source that readers can see for themselves. If that ever changes, we will publish the rules here before we use one.

## What we store and what we publish

For every source behind a story, we store and publish in the story's evidence file:

- the address (URL) and the publisher;
- the time we retrieved it;
- a cryptographic hash of the page as retrieved, a fingerprint that shows what the source said at that moment;
- the claims we took from it, each with the short passage that supports it.

We don't publish full copies of anyone else's work. To check our stories and re-check them after publication, our systems keep a private working copy of source pages. That copy is never published and is kept only as long as we need it for verification and corrections.

## When a source changes

We re-check the sources behind each story 6 hours, 1 day, 7 days and 30 days after publication. If a source is corrected, changed or withdrawn, an editor reviews our story and we act under our Corrections policy.
