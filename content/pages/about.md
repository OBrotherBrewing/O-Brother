{{ brand.name }} publishes verified progress news. It is for people who want to stay informed without the emotional cost of doomscrolling: people who care about the world, who don't want to be told that everything is fine, and who are tired of finishing the news feeling worse and knowing less.

Our promise is simple: **know what is improving without pretending the world is perfect.**

## What we are

We report on things that have measurably got better, and we show our working. Every story links to its sources, explains why it matters, and sets out the evidence and its limits. If the evidence is thin, we say so. If it is too thin, we don't run the story.

## What counts as a story

"Positive" is not a writing style here. It is a classification we give a story only when the evidence supports it. A story qualifies if it shows at least one of these:

- **Something measurably improved.** A rate fell, a figure rose or an outcome got better, and we can show the numbers.
- **A credible solution worked.** A programme, policy or technology produced measured results, not just promises.
- **A harmful trend reversed.** Something that was getting worse has turned, and the data shows it.
- **A meaningful human achievement.** Something a person or group did that we can verify and report without exploiting anyone.
- **Knowledge advanced.** Research moved understanding forward, reported at the stage it has actually reached.
- **A community benefited.** People in a particular place are better off in a way we can describe and check.
- **Justified hope.** There are solid, evidence-based reasons to expect improvement, and we explain both the reasons and the uncertainty.

If a story doesn't meet one of these tests, we don't publish it, however pleasant it sounds.

## What we don't do

- **No saccharine spin.** We don't dress up modest news as a triumph or cheer every announcement. Our headlines say what the evidence says and no more.
- **No partisan cheerleading.** We report progress wherever it comes from. We don't take sides between parties or campaigns, and good news is not a reward for anyone's politics.
- **No tragedy-laundering.** We won't turn a disaster into a feel-good story by leading with the silver lining and burying the harm. When difficult context matters, it stays in.
- **No unverified viral anecdotes.** A heart-warming post shared a million times is not evidence. If we can't verify it, we don't run it.
- **No rewriting other outlets' journalism.** Where another newsroom broke a story, we credit and link to it, confirm the facts from primary sources, and write our own report. We never paraphrase another publisher's article wholesale.

## How to read one of our stories

Every story is built the same way, so you can judge it for yourself:

- the outcome in one sentence, with the key number where there is one;
- **Why this matters**, in two or three sentences;
- **Evidence and limitations**, covering what we don't know yet and what could change;
- the sources, with links;
- an evidence file listing each factual claim beside the passage in the source that supports it;
- a note on how the story was produced, including who reviewed it;
- any corrections, dated, at the foot of the page.
